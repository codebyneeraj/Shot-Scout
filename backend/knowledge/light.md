# Light

## Golden hour
tags: golden_hour, exterior, sunset, sunrise

- The hour after sunrise and before sunset; low, warm, soft, directional sun.
- Backlight the subject (sun behind them) for rim light and haze; expose for the sky for silhouettes.
- Front-lit at golden hour gives warm, flattering skin with long shadows; side-light gives shape and mood.
- The light changes every few minutes: shoot the wides that show the sky first, then close-ups (which can be faked later with a bounce).
- Sunset is fast at low latitudes (near the equator) and slow at high latitudes. Plan 2–3 setups at most.

## Blue hour and dusk
tags: blue_hour, exterior, dusk, dawn, twilight

- About 20–40 minutes after sunset (or before sunrise): soft, cool, even light with a deep blue sky.
- Best for city lights, practicals and silhouettes against the sky; balance practical lights with the sky.
- Exposure drops fast: raise ISO in steps and open the aperture; lock white balance (do not use auto).
- "Day-for-dusk": if blue hour is too short, shoot wides at blue hour and close-ups later with lights.

## Harsh midday sun
tags: harsh_sun, exterior, day, noon

- Sun high overhead gives hard top-light, dark eye sockets and squinting. Avoid close-ups on faces in open sun.
- Fixes: move the actor into open shade, turn their back to the sun (backlight) and bounce light into the face, use a diffusion frame (scrim) overhead.
- Midday suits wides of landscapes, graphic hard shadows, heat, exposure-to-the-elements stories.
- Needs strong ND to keep a wide aperture at 180° shutter.

## Overcast
tags: overcast, exterior, rain, cloudy

- A giant softbox: soft, even, low-contrast light. Great for faces and for matching shots over a long day.
- Flat skies look dull in wides: frame the sky out, or embrace it for melancholy and grief.
- Add contrast with negative fill (black flag on one side of the face).
- Rain: backlight the rain to make it visible; front-lit rain disappears on camera.

## Night exterior
tags: night, exterior

- Without a lighting kit, use practicals: street lamps, shop windows, car headlights, phone screens.
- Pick locations by their existing light; shoot wide open and accept grain over darkness.
- Wet the street to double the practical light in reflections.
- If there is no light at all, suggest shooting at blue hour instead (day-for-night alternative).

## Interior day
tags: interior, day

- Motivate light from the windows: put the actor side-lit by the window for shape, not flat front light.
- Watch the window exposure: either let it blow out (dreamy) or add ND gel/bring the interior up.
- Bounce fill from the window side to soften shadows.

## Interior night
tags: interior, night

- Light from practicals in frame (lamps, candles, screens, fridge light); tungsten is warm (~3200K).
- Low-key: let most of the room fall into darkness, light only faces and key props.
- Candle or firelight: keep the source in frame or close, flicker it, very shallow focus.
