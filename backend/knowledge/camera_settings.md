# Camera settings

## Frame rate and shutter
tags: core

- 24 fps for a cinematic look; shutter 1/50 (180° rule: shutter is about 1/(2 × fps)).
- 48–60 fps for slow motion (shutter 1/100 or 1/120); 120 fps for extreme slow motion if the camera allows.
- 25 fps / 1/50 for PAL broadcast regions; 30 fps / 1/60 for online or NTSC.
- Faster shutter (1/100+) at 24 fps gives a choppy, staccato look for action; slower is rarely useful.

## Exposure and ND
tags: core

- Keep the shutter at 180° and control brightness with ND filters, not the shutter.
- Each 0.3 of ND = 1 stop. ND 0.6 = 2 stops, 0.9 = 3, 1.2 = 4, 1.8 = 6.
- Bright sun with a wide aperture (f/1.8–f/2.8) usually needs ND 1.2–1.8; golden hour 0.6–0.9; overcast 0.3–0.9; blue hour and night none.
- Use the lowest ISO that exposes correctly; on phones stay at or below ISO 800 to avoid noise; on cinema cameras use the native ISO.

## White balance
tags: core

- Set it manually; never leave it on auto during a take.
- Tungsten / practical lamps ~3200K; golden hour ~4500–5600K (set 5600K to keep the warmth); daylight 5600K; overcast/shade 6000–7000K; blue hour 5000–5600K (lower to make it bluer); sodium street lights ~2700K.

## Aperture and depth of field
tags: core

- Shallow (f/1.4–f/2.8): close-ups, intimacy, separating subject from busy background.
- Medium (f/4–f/5.6): mediums and two-shots, keeps both faces sharp.
- Deep (f/8+): landscapes, architecture, wides where everything matters.

## Picture profile
tags: core

- Log or flat profile (Blackmagic Film, S-Log3, Apple Log, etc.) when you will color grade; it protects highlights in high-contrast light.
- Standard/Rec.709 for no-grade quick work or very low light on phones (log adds noise in the dark).
- 4K for flexibility to crop and stabilise; 16:9 by default, 2.39:1 framing guides for a wider cinematic look.

## Phone cameras (e.g. Blackmagic Camera app)
tags: core

- Lock exposure, focus and white balance before rolling.
- Phones have fixed apertures: control exposure with ND filters and ISO; shallow focus comes from distance to background and the tele lens, not the aperture.
- Use the main (wide) lens for low light; the tele and ultra-wide lenses are noisier.
