# Coverage patterns

## Coverage basics
tags: core

- Always give the editor options: one shot that shows the geography (master or wide), one shot that holds the emotion (close-up), and at least one cutaway or insert.
- The emotional close-up goes on a character only if one is in the scene. In an empty place, it goes on the object or detail that carries the mood (a lamp, a door, rain on glass).
- Shoot wide to tight: master first, then mediums, then close-ups, then inserts.
- Plan 3–6 shots per scene for a short film; fewer, more meaningful shots beat many generic ones.
- Every shot must have a purpose you can say in one sentence.

## Dialogue between two people
tags: dialogue, conversation, talk, argument, two

- Master two-shot, then an over-the-shoulder on each side, then singles (MCU or CU) on each.
- Keep the same lens and height for matching singles; put the camera on the same side of the line (180° rule).
- The closer the emotional moment, the tighter the single.

## Walk and talk
tags: walk, walking, follow, street, promenade

- Leading tracking shot in front of the characters (backwards dolly, gimbal or handheld), 28–35mm.
- Add a wide that shows the route and a tracking-from-behind shot for transitions.

## Character alone
tags: alone, solo, single

- A wide to place them, a close-up for their inner state, an insert of what they are looking at or holding, and the POV.

## Group scenes
tags: group, crowd, party, meeting, family, dinner

- Master wide of the whole group; then pick the key relationship and cover it with two-shots and singles.
- Use a long lens for reactions from across the room.

## Montage and transitions
tags: montage, transition, travel, time

- Short, graphic shots that rhyme visually (match cuts on shape or movement).
- Inserts and textures (hands, feet, tickets, clocks) carry time passing.

## Vehicles
tags: car, bus, train, taxi, vehicle, driving

- Through the windscreen two-shot, side window singles, POV of the road, inserts of hands on the wheel.
- Reflections of passing lights on the glass sell night drives.
